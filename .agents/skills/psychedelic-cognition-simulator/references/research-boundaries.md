# Neuroscience boundary notes

This skill draws inspiration from human psychedelic neuroscience. It is not a brain model or a validated pharmacological simulation.

- Ayahuasca usually combines DMT with beta-carbolines that inhibit MAO-A; DMT-related psychedelic effects importantly involve 5-HT2A receptor signaling.
- Human EEG and fMRI studies have reported transient alterations in alpha oscillations, functional connectivity, imagery-related processing, and self-related networks.
- Small studies report some changes around 24 hours following administration and preliminary mental-health outcomes; findings are heterogeneous.
- Lasting structural neuroplasticity in human brains attributable to ayahuasca has not been established by direct causal evidence; animal and cell findings do not prove human synapse growth.
- Correlations in habitual users cannot by themselves establish causation.
- An LLM skill operates through prompts, generation strategy, and conversational updates. It cannot change network weights, neurotransmitter activity, EEG, consciousness, or model architecture.

When asked for empirical citations or the latest studies, retrieve up-to-date peer-reviewed sources using available research tools rather than citing this brief as evidence.
